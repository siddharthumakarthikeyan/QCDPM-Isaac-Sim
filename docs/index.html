Quadrotor-Based Mobile Cable-Driven Parallel Manipulator

Siddharth Umakarthikeyan

SASTRA Deemed to be University, India

Doctoral Thesis, 2025 · Supervised by Dr. Badri Narayanan R

Model Code Papers ROS / CoppeliaSim BibTeX

QCDPM moves a 6-DoF platform with four quadrotors and four ground robots on eight winched cables, so the workspace travels with the machine.

Abstract

A cable-driven parallel robot whose anchors are themselves robots has no fixed frame, and its workspace travels with it. We present a physics-accurate model of such a machine in NVIDIA Isaac Sim: four quadrotors as upper anchors and four differential-drive ground robots as lower anchors carry a 6-DoF platform on eight winched cables. The platform is moved in three ways at once: the robots move, the winches change the cable lengths, and the cable tensions are redistributed.

The system is modelled from the single cable up, with cable slack, winch drum dynamics, rotor dynamics and ground-robot traction, on PhysX at 1 kHz. We prove that straight cables leave the platform without yaw authority (structure matrix rank 5) while crossed cables give full 6-DoF control, that the crossing can be changed while the system runs, and that drone thrust and tilt limits act as tension bounds. Every analytical prediction is tested in simulation. A hybrid tension/length winch scheme remains stable where all-length control diverges, and a C++17 / Eigen core closes the loop from simulated sensors with error-state estimation, an exactly solved bounded tension distribution and 3-D team path planning. The system tracks a 3-D figure-eight, plans the whole team around obstacles, stacks blocks with 1.7 mm mean placement error, paints an aircraft wing and 3D-prints a 51 m continuous spiral.

System

Method

  • Platform control. Inverse kinematics and a closed-form, bounded, damped tension distribution at 250 Hz.
  • Hybrid cable control. Drone winches regulate tension, ground winches regulate length, and the pose loop is closed with a wrench PID.
  • Reconfigurable cable layout. Each platform corner is wired straight to its own robot or crossed to the neighbouring robot; the crossing angle changes while the system runs.
  • Formation. The robots hold position while the platform is inside the region the winches alone can serve, and move only as far as needed when it leaves.
  • Team path planning. RRT* with pruning and Bézier smoothing; ground robots and all eight cables are checked against obstacles.
  • Estimation (C++ core). IMU-driven error-state filter per drone, odometry with pose fixes per ground robot, and a platform observer driven by measured cable tensions and corrected by cable lengths and AprilTag poses, which also estimates the payload.
  • Control (C++ core). SE(3) tracking wrench; bounded tension distribution solved exactly under winch, drone tilt and thrust, and ground-robot sliding and tipping limits; geometric drone control with cable feed-forward; closed-loop eigenvalue stability check.
  • 3-D planning (C++ core). Elevation map from drone depth cameras and ground-robot lidars, 3-D A* for the whole team, re-planned while moving.
  • ROS 2. Platform commands and ground truth, per-cable length, rate and tension, tension-distribution output and per-robot odometry and IMU, on simulation time.

Results

Limitations

This is a simulation and has not been run on hardware. By default the controllers use ground-truth poses; with the C++ core they run from sensor models, and rendered camera images and lidar scans are published but not processed in the loop. Dense builds are not solved: in the enclosure, 19 of 42 blocks are more than 10 mm off, and the dome pattern does not stand. Physics runs at about 0.3× real time. Paint film, printed bead and supply hose are drawn along the recorded tool path for the renders and put no load on the platform.
